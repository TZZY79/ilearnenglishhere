Lesson 11 · Grammar · Noun Phrases

🔢 Quantity: much, many, a little, a few, a lot of

Know exactly which word to use — this is one of the most common grammar tests at B1!

📊 Reference Table

WordCountable ✅Uncountable ✅When?
much❌✅Negatives & questions mainly
many✅❌Negatives & questions mainly
a little❌✅Small amount — positive
a few✅❌Small number — positive
a lot of / lots of✅✅All sentences — safe choice!

🔑 The COUNT TEST — Never Get This Wrong Again!

Ask yourself: Can I count this? → 1 apple, 2 apples? → YES ✅ = COUNTABLE → use many / a few
Water? → 1 water, 2 waters? → NO ❌ = UNCOUNTABLE → use much / a little
Not sure? → use a lot of — it works for BOTH types every time! 🛡️
⚠️ "much" in positive statements sounds strange → "I have much time" ❌ → "I have a lot of time" ✅
💧 "There isn't much water." → water = uncountable ✅
📚 "There aren't many books." → books = countable ✅
🧂 "Add a little salt." → salt = uncountable, positive ✅
👫 "A few friends came to my party." → friends = countable, positive ✅
🎵 "I listen to a lot of music." + "I know a lot of songs." ✅ both!
🎯 Activity 1 — Choose the Correct Word

🎯 Activity 2 — Error Correction

❌ much friends → ✅ many friends
"Friends" is countable (1 friend, 2 friends) → must use MANY ✅
🎯 Activity 3 — Fill in the Blank

Lesson 12 · Grammar · Future

🔮 Future: going to

Use "going to" for future plans and intentions that were already decided before the moment of speaking.

📐 Formula

✅ +  Subject + am/is/are + going to + BASE VERB
❌ −  Subject + am/is/are + NOT + going to + BASE VERB
❓ ?  Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + BASE VERB?
🛫 "I am going to travel to Thailand next month." → planned in advance ✅
🍳 "She is going to cook dinner tonight." → she/he/it → IS ✅
🚫 "We are not going to be late." → negative form ✅
❓ "Are you going to attend the ceremony?" → question form ✅

🔑 Going To vs Will — Quick Decision Guide

🔮 Going to = plan ALREADY DECIDED before now → "I'm going to visit grandma this Sunday." (decided last week) 📅
⚡ Will = decision made RIGHT NOW in the moment → "OK I'll carry your bag!" 🎒
⚠️ Never say "is going to went" — after "going to" always BASE VERB: "is going to go" ✅
🎯 Activity 1 — Choose the Correct Form
1. "He ________ study engineering — he already applied!" 🔧
🎯 Activity 2 — Make a Question
2. Rewrite as a question: "They are going to open a new restaurant." ❓🍽️
✅ "Are they going to open a new restaurant?"
Move "are" to the front of the sentence: Are + subject + going to + base verb?
🎯 Activity 3 — Error Correction
3. Fix the mistake: "She is going to went to the market tomorrow." 🛒
🎯 Activity 4 — Fill in the Blank
4. "________ she going to attend the seminar next week?"
✅ Is — "she" is singular (third person) → Is she going to...? ✅

Lesson 15 · Grammar · Auxiliary Verbs

💼 Have to / Don't have to

Express necessity and lack of necessity — key for workplace and everyday situations.

📐 Formula

✅ Obligation:   Subject + have/has to + BASE VERB
❌ Not needed: Subject + don't/doesn't have to + BASE VERB
❓ Question:   Do/Does + subject + have to + BASE VERB?
📋 "I have to submit this report today." → it is necessary ✅
👔 "She has to wear a uniform." → she/he/it → HAS (not have!) ✅
😎 "You don't have to stay late." → not required, your choice ✅
❓ "Does he have to attend the meeting?" → question ✅

⚠️ Critical Difference: Must Not vs Don't Have To

Must not / Mustn't = FORBIDDEN — it is absolutely NOT allowed 🚫
Don't have to = NOT NECESSARY — you can choose to do it if you want 🆓
"You must not cheat in the exam." = If you do, there will be serious consequences! 🚫
"You don't have to bring lunch." = We provide food, but bring your own if you prefer 🍱
🎯 Activity 1
1. "She ________ bring her own textbook — the college provides them." (not necessary) 📖
🎯 Activity 2 — Fill in the Blank
2. "________ students have to wear ID badges at your college?"
✅ Do — "students" = plural → Do (not Does). "Does" is for he/she/it. ✅
🎯 Activity 3 — Multiple Choice
3. "You ________ talk during the exam. It's against the rules!" 🚫🗣️

Lesson 16 · Grammar · Auxiliary Verbs

✅ Should / Shouldn't

Give advice and recommendations — one of the highest-frequency structures in everyday English!

📐 Formula

✅ +  Subject + should + BASE VERB   (NO "to"!)
❌ −  Subject + shouldn't + BASE VERB
❓ ?  Should + subject + BASE VERB?
💊 "You should take medicine." → good advice ✅
🚬 "You shouldn't smoke." → bad idea ❌
❓ "Should I call a doctor?" → asking for advice ❓
🌙 "You shouldn't stay up too late." → negative advice ✅

🔑 Critical Rule — No "to", no "s"!

After "should/shouldn't" → BASE VERB only (no "to", no "s", no "-ing")
✅ "She should go" | ❌ "She should to go" | ❌ "She should goes"
Should is WEAKER than "have to" — it's advice, not a rule or law!
"should" = good idea · "shouldn't" = bad idea · "have to" = it's a RULE 📋

✅ "You shouldn't eat cold food." 🍦

⏰ Since / For / Ago / From…To / Until

Master these time expressions — they appear in almost every part of the B1 exam!

📌 Core Reference Table

WordUseExample
sinceSpecific starting POINT in time"since 2020 / since Monday"
forDURATION — length of time"for 3 years / for 2 hours"
agoDistance BEFORE NOW — past simple"3 years ago / 10 minutes ago"
from…toStart time → End time"from 8am to 5pm"
untilContinuing up to a point then stopping"until Friday / until I arrive"

🔑 SINCE vs FOR — The Ultimate Memory Trick

🔵 SINCE = a specific POINT → a year, a date, a day, "I was young", "I started college"
🟢 FOR = a PERIOD of time → "3 years", "2 hours", "a long time", "six months"
Quick test: Is it countable as a DURATION? → FOR | Is it a specific MOMENT? → SINCE
🔴 AGO = always goes at the END and uses PAST SIMPLE, not present perfect!
"She moved here 3 years ago." ✅ | "She moved here since 3 years." ❌

⭐ Special Student Request · Grammar

🔄 Present Perfect Continuous

Students asked for this — and it's one of the most important structures for B1 and beyond! Master it here.

📌 When to Use the PPC

  • 1️⃣ An action that STARTED in the past and is STILL happening NOW 🔄
  • 2️⃣ An action that recently STOPPED but has visible results right now 👀
  • 3️⃣ To emphasise the DURATION or CONTINUITY of an action ⏳

📐 The Formula — 3 Parts — Always All 3!

✅ +  Subject + have/has + been + verb-ING
❌ −  Subject + haven't/hasn't been + verb-ING
❓ ?  Have/Has + subject + been + verb-ING?
📚 "I have been studying for 3 hours." → still studying right now ✅
🍳 "She has been cooking since 2pm." → she/he/it = HAS ✅
❓ "How long have you been waiting?" → question ✅
😴 "He hasn't been sleeping well." → negative ✅

🆚 PPC vs Present Perfect Simple

PPC (have been -ing)PP Simple (have -ed / irregular)
Focus on DURATION / PROCESS 🔄Focus on COMPLETION / RESULT ✅
"I have been reading all day." (still going)"I have read the book." (finished!)
"She has been working here for 2 years.""She has worked here." (at some point)
Your hands are wet → "Have you been washing?" 🫧"Have you washed the dishes?" (done or not?)

🔑 PPC Cheat Codes — Memorise These!

Formula shortcut: HAVE/HAS + BEEN + -ING — all 3 parts, always!
He/She/It/Everyone → HAS been | I/You/We/They → HAVE been
Key signal words: "for", "since", "how long", "all day", "lately", "recently" → think PPC!
PPC = DURATION focus | PP Simple = RESULT focus
⚠️ Common error: "He have been study" ❌ → "He has been studying" ✅
🎯 Activity 1 — Complete the Sentence
1. "They ________ (wait) for 45 minutes! The bus is so late! 😤" — Use PPC.
🎯 Activity 2 — Fill in the Blank
2. "She __________ (live) in Phnom Penh since 2018."
✅ has been living — She = singular → HAS + been + live-ING ✅
🎯 Activity 3 — Full Error Correction

✅ "He has been studying here for 3 years."
• have → has (he = singular)
• study → studying (PPC requires -ING)
• since 3 years → for 3 years (duration of time = FOR, not SINCE)

🗣️ Modal Verbs — Complete Master Guide

Students asked for a dedicated modal verbs section! Modal verbs are in every part of the exam. Master them ALL here.

📌 The Golden Rule of ALL Modal Verbs

After every modal verb → BASE VERB only (no "to", no "s", no "-ing")
✅ "She can speak Khmer." | ❌ "She can speaks" | ❌ "She can to speak"

📊 Complete Modal Verb Table

ModalMeaning / UseExample 🌟
🔵 canAbility / Permission (present)"I can swim." 🏊
🟢 couldPast ability / Polite request"Could you help me?" 🙏
🟡 shouldAdvice / Recommendation"You should rest." 😴
🟠 have toNecessity / Obligation (rule)"I have to attend." 📋
🔴 must / must notStrong obligation / Forbidden"You must not cheat." 🚫
🟣 willFuture — instant/spontaneous decision"I'll help you!" ⚡
⚪ wouldPolite / Conditional"I would like coffee." ☕
🔵 might / mayPossibility — not certain"It might rain." 🌧️

🔑 Modal Verb Master Cheat Codes

1. Never add "s" to modal verbs → "She can" ✅ | "She cans" ❌
2. Never use "to" after most modals → "should go" ✅ | "should to go" ❌
3. EXCEPTION: "have TO" always uses "to" — this is different from other modals!
4. Must not = FORBIDDEN 🚫 | Don't have to = not necessary 🆓 (totally different!)
5. Could/Would = more polite than can/will → use in formal/respectful situations 🤝
6. Might/May = possibility (50% or less certain) → "It might rain." (I'm not sure) 🌧️
