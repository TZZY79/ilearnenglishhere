Present Perfect Tense

✦ Formula

Subject + have / has + Past Participle

✦ Examples

I have called the clinic three times.
She has already made an appointment.
They haven't seen a doctor yet.
Have you been to the doctor this year?

✦ Key Time Words

ever never already yet just since for
When Do We Use Present Perfect?

✔ Use it for…

  • Experience (ever/never):
    I have never had surgery.
  • Recent actions (just):
    I've just taken my temperature.
  • Unfinished situations (since/for):
    She has been sick since Monday.

✗ Do NOT use for…

  • Specific times:
    I went yesterday. ✔
    I have gone yesterday. ✗
  • Clock times or dates:
    She called at 3pm. ✔
  • Words: yesterday, ago, last week, in 2019
💡 Memory Trick: Ask yourself — "Can I say WHEN exactly?" If YES → Simple Past. If NO → Present Perfect.
Dialogue: Making an Appointment
Receptionist
Good morning, City Clinic. How can I help you?
Patient
Hello, I'd like to make an appointment. I have been feeling unwell for a few days.
Receptionist

Patient
No, I haven't. I haven't had time yet.

We have an available slot on Thursday at 10 a.m. Does that work?

Too and Enough

✦ TOO = more than necessary (problem!)

too + adjective / adverb
This coffee is too hot to drink.
You are brushing too hard — it damages gums.

✦ ENOUGH = sufficient

adjective + enough  |  enough + noun
I don't floss enough. (not sufficient)
Is the anaesthetic strong enough?
💡 Position Rule: TOO comes BEFORE the adjective. ENOUGH comes AFTER the adjective but BEFORE a noun.
"too expensive" ✔   "strong enough" ✔   "enough time" ✔
Want / Would Like + Object + Infinitive
want / would like + [PERSON] + to + base verb
I want the dentist to check my tooth.
She would like you to explain the treatment.
The doctor wants me to floss every day.
Without object: I want to book an appointment.
💡 Politeness: "Would like" is more polite than "want." Use it when speaking to doctors, receptionists, and pharmacists.

Past Continuous Tense
Subject + was / were + verb-ing

✦ Use it for…

An action in progress at a point in the past:
At 9pm, I was talking to my therapist.
A background action interrupted by another:
I was dreaming when the alarm went off.

✦ Negative & Question

She wasn't sleeping well last month.
Were you feeling anxious during the session?
Past Continuous + When / While Clauses

✦ Short action interrupts long action → WHEN

was/were + -ing   WHEN   simple past
I was reading when the phone rang.
She was walking home when it started raining.

✦ Two long simultaneous actions → WHILE

was/were + -ing   WHILE   was/were + -ing
I was dreaming while she was sleeping beside me.
💡 Movie trick: Past continuous = slow camera shot (scene-setting). Simple past = the sudden cut (event that happens).
Model Paragraph: A Strange Dream

Last night, I had the strangest dream. I was walking through a dark forest when I suddenly heard music. While I was trying to find the source, a lion appeared from behind a tree. I was running away when I woke up. My heart was beating very fast.

Must vs. Have To — The Key Difference

MUST

  • Comes from the speaker's own feeling
  • Personal or internal obligation
  • I must study harder.
  • You must see a doctor. (my advice)
  • No past form → use had to

HAVE TO

  • Comes from an external rule
  • Someone else makes the rule
  • You have to show your prescription.
  • Patients have to wait in line.
  • Has past: had to, future: will have to
⚠ Critical: MUSTN'T = forbidden (do NOT do it!)  |  DON'T HAVE TO = not necessary (your choice). These are OPPOSITES!
Dialogue: At the Pharmacy
Customer
I have a prescription for antibiotics.
Pharmacist
Of course. You must take one tablet three times a day. You mustn't drink alcohol while taking these.
Customer
Do I have to take them with food?
Pharmacist
You should take them with food to avoid nausea, but you don't have to.
Customer
Are there any side effects I should know about?

Unit 4 — Grammar Summary

Tenses

  • Present Perfect: have/has + past participle
  • PP vs SP: ever/since vs. yesterday/ago
  • Past Continuous: was/were + -ing
  • When clause: -ing + when + simple past

Other Structures

  • Too: too + adjective (problem)
  • Enough: adj + enough (sufficient)
  • Want/WL + object + to + verb
  • Modals: must, should, have to, mustn't, don't have to
🎯 Exam tip: Look for the TIME WORD first — it tells you which tense to use!
