📐 Grammar – Lesson 12

🔮 Future: going to

📌 When to Use "going to"

✅ Use for future PLANS or INTENTIONS already decided 🎯
✅ "I am going to study medicine." (I already decided this!)
✅ "She is going to cook dinner tonight." (already planned!)

📐 The Formula

Subject + am/is/are + going to + INFINITIVE (base verb)
➕ I am going to travel. | She is going to call.
➖ I am not going to travel. | She isn't going to call.
❓ Are you going to travel? | Is she going to call?

✅ Positive Examples

🛫 "We are going to fly to Bangkok."
🎂 "He is going to bake a cake."
📚 "I am going to study tonight."

❌ Negative Examples

🚗 "I am not going to drive."
🎮 "She isn't going to play games."
🏃 "They aren't going to run."
🔑 Cheat Code: "going to" = already DECIDED. "will" = decided NOW in the moment. Example: "I am going to travel next week" (planned last month) vs "I will carry your bag!" (just decided now) 💡

📐 Grammar – Lesson 15

💼 Auxiliary Verbs: have to / don't have to

📌 Meaning

✅ Have to = It is NECESSARY / OBLIGATORY 📋
✅ Don't have to = It is NOT NECESSARY (but you can if you want) 🆓
⚠️ Don't have to ≠ Must not! "Must not" = FORBIDDEN. "Don't have to" = just not required.

📐 Formula

✅ Positive: Subject + have/has to + infinitive
❌ Negative: Subject + don't/doesn't + have to + infinitive
❓ Question: Do/Does + subject + have to + infinitive?

✅ Examples: HAVE TO

📋 "Students have to wear uniform." (necessary!)
👩‍💼 "She has to finish the report." (she/he/it → HAS)
⏰ "I have to wake up at 6am."

❌ Examples: DON'T HAVE TO

😎 "You don't have to wear a tie."
🏖️ "She doesn't have to work on Sundays."
🚗 "We don't have to drive — it's close!"
🔑 He/She/It Rule: Always use HAS to (not have) with he, she, it. → "He has to study." ✅ | "He have to study." ❌

📐 Grammar – Lesson 16

✅ Modal Verb: should / shouldn't

📌 Meaning

✅ Should = It's a good idea / advice / recommendation 💡
❌ Shouldn't = It's NOT a good idea / bad advice 🚫
⚠️ Should is WEAKER than have to — it's advice, not a rule!

📐 Formula

✅ Positive: Subject + should + infinitive (no "to")
❌ Negative: Subject + should + not (shouldn't) + infinitive
❓ Question: Should + subject + infinitive?

✅ SHOULD Examples

💊 "You should take medicine." (advice)
💤 "He should sleep more."
🥗 "We should eat healthily."

❌ SHOULDN'T Examples

🚬 "You shouldn't smoke." (bad idea!)
🌙 "He shouldn't stay up late."
🍔 "She shouldn't eat junk food."
🔑 Key Rule: After "should/shouldn't", the verb is ALWAYS bare infinitive (no "to", no "s"). ✅ "should go" ❌ NOT "should to go" / "should goes"

📐 Grammar – Lessons 17–18

⏰ Time Words: since / for / ago / from…to / until

📌 Core Meanings

WordMeaningExample
⏰ sinceWhen something STARTED (and still continues now)"I've lived here since 2020."
⌛ forHOW LONG something lasts (a duration)"I've studied for 3 years."
🔙 agoHow far BEFORE NOW something happened (past)"I arrived 2 days ago."
↔️ from…toStart time TO end time"I work from 8am to 5pm."
🛑 untilUp to the point in time when something stops"Wait until I arrive."
🔑 SINCE vs FOR Memory Trick:
SINCE = a specific POINT in time → 2020, Monday, last week, I was 5 years old 📍
FOR = a PERIOD of time → 3 years, 2 hours, a long time ⏱️
Ask: "Is it a POINT or a PERIOD?" → That decides your answer!
📐 Grammar – Time Words

⏰ Since / For – Examples & Context

📍 SINCE = specific starting point

📅 "I've studied English since 2019."
📅 "She's lived in Phnom Penh since January."
📅 "He's been sick since Monday."
📅 "We've been friends since we were kids."

⏱️ FOR = duration (length of time)

🕐 "I've studied English for 5 years."
🕐 "She worked there for 6 months."
🕐 "He's been sick for 3 days."
🕐 "We've been friends for a long time."

🔙 AGO – Used with PAST SIMPLE (finished action)

⚠️ AGO always goes at the END of the time expression
📅 "I moved here 3 years ago." (= 3 years before NOW)
📅 "She called me 10 minutes ago."
📅 "They met 5 years ago."

⭐ Special Focus – Present Perfect Continuous

🔄 Present Perfect Continuous (PPC) – Deep Dive!

📌 When to Use PPC

1️⃣ An action that STARTED in the past and is STILL happening NOW 🔄
2️⃣ An action RECENTLY STOPPED but with visible results now 👀
3️⃣ To emphasise the DURATION or CONTINUITY of an action ⏳

📐 The Formula

Subject + have/has + been + verb-ING
➕ "I have been studying English for 5 years."
➖ "She hasn't been sleeping well lately."
❓ "How long have you been waiting?"
🔑 EASY MEMORY FORMULA:
have/has + been + verb-ING
Think: "HAVE BEEN = past started | ING = still going on!" 🎯
He → HAS been | I/You/We/They → HAVE been
⭐ Present Perfect Continuous

🔄 PPC vs Present Perfect Simple — What's the Difference?

🔄 PPC – Focus on DURATION/PROCESS

📖 "I have been reading this book all day." (still reading, or just finished)
🏋️ "She has been exercising since morning."
😴 "He has been sleeping for 10 hours."

✅ PP Simple – Focus on COMPLETION/RESULT

📖 "I have read this book." (finished! ✅)
🏋️ "She has exercised today." (completed)
😴 "He has slept well." (result: feels rested)

🎭 Real Context Examples

💧 "Your hands are wet. Have you been washing them?" (PPC — the process)
💧 "Have you washed the dishes?" (PP Simple — done or not?)
📝 "I have been writing this essay for 3 hours." (still going)
📝 "I have written the essay." (it's done!)
⭐ Present Perfect Continuous

🔄 PPC + Since / For = Perfect Partners! 💑

PPC LOVES since and for! 🎉

⏱️ "I have been studying for 3 hours." (duration = FOR)
📍 "I have been studying since 9am." (starting point = SINCE)
⏱️ "She has been working here for 2 years."
📍 "He has been living in Phnom Penh since 2019."
⏱️ "They have been waiting for 30 minutes." 😤

🔑 PPC CHEAT CODE 🔥

Formula: have/has + been + VERB-ING
With "how long?" questions → always use PPC!
"How long HAVE you BEEN WORKING here?" ✅
Signs of PPC: "lately", "recently", "all day", "for + time", "since + point"
He/She/It = HAS been | I/You/We/They = HAVE been

🔢 QUANTITY CHEAT CODE

❓ Can you COUNT it? → YES = many / a few | NO = much / a little
✅ Works ALWAYS: a lot of / lots of (countable AND uncountable!)
⚠️ MUCH = usually in negatives & questions ONLY (not positive statements)
Rule: "I have MUCH time" ❌ → "I have A LOT OF time" ✅

🍳 COOKING VERB ORDER

🥔 Peel → 🔪 Slice → ➕ Add → 🛢️ Fry → ♨️ Boil → 🔥 Roast → 🥚 Beat
"Please Stop All Food Before Really Baking" = Peel Slice Add Fry Boil Roast Beat

😊 FEELING ADJECTIVES — NEVER SAY "BORING PERSON"!

😑 Bored = the person FEELS bored (I am bored) ✅
😑 Boring = the thing/person CAUSES boredom (The lesson is boring) ✅
Same for: excited/exciting | frightened/frightening | surprised/surprising | worried/worrying

⭐ Special Focus – Modal Verbs

🗣️ Modal Verbs – Complete Master Guide!

📌 What ARE Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are HELPER verbs that show how CERTAIN, NECESSARY, or POSSIBLE something is.
⚠️ Golden Rule: After modal verbs, ALWAYS use BASE VERB (no "to", no "s", no "-ing")
✅ "She can speak Khmer." ❌ NOT "She can speaks" | NOT "She can to speak"
ModalMeaningExample
🔵 canAbility / permission"I can swim." 🏊
🟢 couldPast ability / polite request"Could you help me?" 🙏
🟡 shouldAdvice / recommendation"You should sleep." 😴
🟠 have toObligation / necessity"I have to study." 📚
🔴 mustStrong obligation / must not = forbidden"You must not cheat." 🚫
🟣 willFuture (instant decision)"I will help!" 🙋
⚪ wouldConditional / polite"I would like a coffee." ☕
🔵 might/mayPossibility (not sure)"It might rain today." 🌧️
⭐ Modal Verbs Rules

🗣️ Modal Verb Rules – No Exceptions!

✅ Correct Modal Structures

She can speak three languages. ✅
You should rest today. ✅
I must finish this. ✅
He might come later. ✅
We could try a new restaurant. ✅

❌ Common Mistakes

She can speaks ❌ → can speak ✅
You should to rest ❌ → should rest ✅
I must to finish ❌ → must finish ✅
He might comes ❌ → might come ✅
She cans swim ❌ → can swim ✅

🔑 MODAL VERB CHEAT CODES

1. Modal verbs NEVER change for he/she/it — no "s"! ✅
2. NEVER use "to" after: can, could, should, must, will, would, might, may
3. EXCEPTION: "have to" — this IS followed by "to"! (have TO + infinitive)
4. Negatives: can't | couldn't | shouldn't | must not | won't | wouldn't | might not
5. Questions: flip the modal and subject! → "Should I...?" "Can you...?" "Must we...?"
