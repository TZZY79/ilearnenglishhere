Real England: what locals really say.

Textbooks teach “How are you? — I'm fine, thank you, and you?” Then you arrive in England and someone says “Alright, bab?”. Follow Linh's story through 16 animated, real-life conversations. Find out what people mean, what they expect you to say, and why it isn't always what you'd expect.

🏝️ England is an island, and the people are unique

England looks like a lot of places: high streets, coffee chains, phones in every hand. But it's part of an island, Great Britain. Centuries of island life shaped how people talk. They're polite but indirect, they love a joke, and every town has its own words. Learn these few secrets and you'll understand the locals much faster, even when the words are new.

1 · Polite but indirect“I'm afraid…”, “Would you mind…?”, “What time were you thinking?” Soft words often carry a clear message.
2 · Sorry, sorry, sorry“Sorry” can be an apology, “excuse me”, “pardon?”, or a polite complaint.
3 · Humour everywhereSarcasm (“Oh, brilliant, rain again”), understatement (“not bad” = good) and friendly teasing (“banter”).
4 · New words every 30 milesLove, duck, bab, pet, mate. Ta, ta-ra, alright? Accents and words change from town to town.
5 · Small talk opens doorsWeather, tea, queues, buses and dogs. These easy topics are how strangers become friends.
6 · Setting changes everything“Hello, gorgeous!” from a market trader is friendly. From a stranger in the street, it isn't. Read the place, the time and the person.
